Here's a game you can play at home. Go through a list of video games consoles sorted by date, take the one that sold the most that generation and then try and find how many outright baffling business moves the manufacturer made around that time that can only be explained by pure hubris. It's an educational insight into how a company that believes it has no competition left makes decisions, and how they determine how much they can dick over consumers before the market punishes them. Nintendo has made their share of dumb boners in the past few decades, and Microsoft can't stop making dumb business moves recently (though how many of those were made due to stupidity or incompetence is hard to say), but in the gaming space there's no company more arrogant than Sony. From the moment they started out in gaming, they tried to screw Nintendo over with the PlayStation's licensing control, and went from there. If you weren't convinced before, then recent news should have.

We're not even talking about [removing movies from people's libraries](https://www.playstation.com/en-gb/legal/psvideocontent/) here; that'd be a minor footnote if not for the news that followed it. Nor are we talking about [shutting down the PlayStation Store for the PS3 and PS Vita](https://blog.playstation.com/2026/07/01/an-update-on-playstation-store-for-ps3-and-ps-vita/), which is at least understandable considering the age of both pieces of hardware. The announcement [Sony will stop manufacturing physical discs for all new releases starting in 2028](https://blog.playstation.com/2026/07/01/physical-disc-production-ending-in-january-2028-for-new-games-releasing-on-playstation-consoles/) set the internet ablaze with gamer fury though, directing all player's eyes to Sony. It's not just a triple whammy, but an incredibly disappointing business move for almost everyone unfortunate enough to still be stuck on PlayStation, although it's admittedly not surprising. Clearly the all-digital future where nobody will own anything and somehow be happy is the kind of wet dream that's been keeping big publishers and most hardware manufacturers going for the past decade, if numerous failed attempts to dabble in cloud gaming and constant claims people aren't buying physical games anymore were anything to go by. But it's pretty obvious Sony was steering their audience in this direction for a while. Just comparing my local prices, the price of a PS5 Digital Edition — without a disc drive — is **€495** (or $565) while a regular one is **€570** (or $650). The PS5 Pro doesn't even have the option; if you want to play physically, you need to buy a separate disc drive for a mere €100 ($115). At that point, it's disingenuous to say this move was made purely due to "consumer preferences" because golly gee, I wonder what option consumers will pick in this economy. On the Nintendo Switch (2), which has no Digital Edition For Poor Plebians, [half of software games are digital](https://insider-gaming.com/nearly-half-of-nintendo-switch-games-sales-are-phyiscal/). Keep in mind this includes the thousands of games that don't have a physical version as well as DLC and such. That's at least enough to make one wonder if physical is as dead as the industry would like you to believe.

![A photo of a GameStop store in a mall](/img/gamespot_mall.jpg)
*Well thank God somebody is trying to keep physical game stores alive. Where else will I get my Marvel Funko Pops?*

And there's plenty of good reasons to be hesitant of moving towards a future where physical isn't an option anymore, even if you primarily buy digitally. Obviously this has already happened to PC gaming, but that's a completely different market for enthusiasts who have money to burn on storage and good internet speeds and are willing to give up physical for benefits that you can't get on consoles, such as modding. Consoles don't have those benefits because they were supposed to be the cheap and convenient option; now they're neither. Just the thought you'll be forced to cough up a hundred buckaroos for a PS6 game that takes an entire day to download, which you can't buy used, sell or even borrow from or to a friend, on a platform where refunding isn't [nearly as convenient as on Steam](https://www.playstation.com/en-us/support/store/ps-store-refund-request/#type), should be enough to make any savvy consumer hesitate, especially if you bought into the reasonable assumption that digital games would trickle savings of not having to manufacture discs down to consumers. At least, it seems reasonable when you consider developers can decide to patch out content at any time ([RIP Billie Jean](https://tcrf.net/Grand_Theft_Auto:_Vice_City/Version_and_Platform_Differences#10th_Anniversary_iOS.2FAndroid_Version), who's not my lover) or even completely wipe their games from stores, but Sony doesn't seem to care that much about preservation if [the loss of *P.T.*](https://www.polygon.com/2015/5/5/8557807/pt-silent-hills-demo-pulled/) is anything to go by. But the cherry on top of this complete lack of sundae is that [the PlayStation Network is neither known for its stability nor its security](https://www.penny-arcade.com/comic/2011/04/29/editorializin), so imagine a future where you can't buy or play any games because the server got hacked for the fiftieth time... this month. Yet Sony is arrogant enough to bet all their chips on this disaster of a network to abandon physical copies in their entirety, when players from over a hundred countries across the world [can't even create an account](https://insider-gaming.com/which-countries-have-psn/) on the stupid thing. That already caused its own minor controversies before due to the requirement of a PSN account to play popular games such as [*Helldivers 2*... **on PC**](https://steamdb.info/sub/137730/history/?changeid=23416542). It's likely a huge part of the backlash comes from players who live somewhere that doesn't have the kinds of internet speeds that moving towards an exclusively digital library requires, or somewhere where they can't create a PSN account. It's not even the first time those players have effectively been told to eat shit by a console manufacturer.

Speaking of the backlash, I'm sure Sony was taken aback by it. They must have had this scheme in the pipeline for a while, and it's obvious what convinced them to pull the metaphorical trigger on the gun pointed at their own metaphorical foot was the response to *Grand Theft Auto VI*. Its release details have been surrounded with some of its own controversy, particularly the part where the publisher hasn't even shown any actual gameplay yet is still trying to convince consumers to [buy the $100 Ultimate Edition over the $80 "Now Shut Up Poor People Edition"](https://www.rockstargames.com/VI). A hundred goddamn dollars just so you can get a download code in a box doesn't seem like much of a luxury compared to [Rockstar's previous $100 options](https://www.reddit.com/r/GTA/comments/1cba53m/grand_theft_auto_iv_special_edition/), but pre-order numbers are still somewhere in the millions, which must have seemed convincing to Sony. Reportedly they [didn't even tell anyone about their plans](https://highchaosrun.com/2026/07/07/playstation-killed-discs-without-telling-its-publishers-or-business-partners/#:~:text=%E2%80%9CDespite%20our%20seemingly%20close%20working%20relationship%20with%20PlayStation%20we%20weren%E2%80%99t%20informed%20that%20this%20was%20happening%2C%E2%80%9D%20says%20one%20executive%20at%20a%20major%20AAA%20publisher.%20%E2%80%9CIt%E2%80%99s%20only%20hitting%20us%20now%20that%20we%20may%20be%20out%20of%20jobs%20sooner%20rather%20than%20later.%E2%80%9D) to halt disc production, which means they didn't just dick over consumers *and* stores, but publishers as well. But when you look at it, Take-Two must have known as well; the timing is just too convenient. GTAVI was clearly used to test the waters of an all-digital PlayStation future, and it confirmed to Sony that even if a lot of consumers would bitch and moan, enough of them would be perfectly willing to forgo physical discs simply because of brand loyalty. They clearly misunderstood where those consumers' loyalties lie; if there's one IP in this industry who can get away with this kind of blatant anti-consumer bullshit, it's *Grand Theft Auto*, which is less of a game and more of a cultural landmark by now. The PlayStation isn't, at least not anymore.

Keep in mind that [the average gamer in the United States is **37 years old**](https://www.gamesmarket.global/21-countries-24216-gamer-industry-associations-led-by-esa-present-global-gamer-study-power-of-play-b6e036fabc44553c6dcbf01d8cf7f7a3/), while the median age in the US is **35 years**. Combine that with the fact that [younger generations are spending less money on games each year](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/new-study-shows-that-gen-z-is-spending-way-less-money-on-videogames-than-older-gamers/), and this recent move from Sony comes across as a company that's aware that there's no growth left in its current model and is trying to squeeze the last bit of blood out of an aging market. Younger generations just don't seem interested in console gaming. Whether it's due to getting priced out of the current generation, the increasing interest in old games or simply being used to "free" phone and tablet games is a mystery, but the irony of the situation can't be denied. The PlayStation 4 sold itself on [its pro-consumer attitude](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kWSIFh8ICaA), in contrast to the Xbox One slapping its metaphorical dick on its userbase's collective face with dumb anti-consumer decisions and [worse justifications](https://youtu.be/J_JVVUnCWnY?t=24), and now Sony is trying to screw over consumers in a very similar way. It seems doubtful this will kill the PlayStation in a similar way though, because there will always be a big enough Gun and Balls market to keep it alive; as long as they get to keep playing [*Fortnite*, *GTA V*, *Minecraft* and *EA Sports FC*](https://newzoo.com/resources/rankings/top-ps5-games), everything is fine. The problem of the PlayStation losing its appeal to anyone outside of that market remains, thoughbeit. This becomes much more obvious when comparing the PS5's sales to its predecessor, as [its sales have been slowing down hard enough](https://www.techspot.com/news/112915-playstation-sales-had-worst-may-25-years-xbox.html) these past few years that it seems doubtful it will ever be able to outsell the PS4. While GTA VI will definitely move a few consoles before they launch the new ones, it probably won't move that many; I imagine most people who were interested in playing GTAVI on day one already own a console that can play it, considering how good price hikes are at instilling FOMO. 

Whether the Gun and Balls market will have much interest in the next generation, however, remains uncertain if the rumors surrounding the PS6's and Xbox Helix' prices have any truth to them. At the very least, Sony claims to have [no interest in selling hardware at a loss](https://www.eurogamer.net/sony-ps6-hardware-significant-loss), so keeping the PS5 Pro's current price in mind, it's likely we'll be looking at launch prices anywhere from twelve to fifteen hundred dollars. Keep in mind consumers would be spending all that money on a new console with a launch library almost assuredly consisting of games that will look and play almost exactly the same on current gen hardware, as it'd be pointless to invest much into next gen exclusives when the industry is in the state that it is. Just consider that you can still buy the latest *EA Sports FC* on PS4 and Xbox One, and that it took until this year's *Call of Duty* installment to stop releasing on those platforms — I've seen many players online express the sentiment that it only feels like the current generation is taking off now, even though the new generation is almost around the corner. That's not even getting into how few notable graphical improvements we've gotten in the past six years: ray tracing, and path tracing in particular (shooting multiple rays per pixel), are clearly still too demanding for real time rendering if game developers have to rely on ugly AI solutions like upscaling and frame generation just to make it look like significant progress is still being made. In reality, it means we're getting games running at a beautiful native 720p (or lower) and a stunning 30fps, except now it's blurrier and laggier than ever before. There's very little incentive for anyone to buy a next generation console at that point.
